Dashboard views

What each view is for

Each view opens on one question. Picking a role — grid operations, renewables, trading or data science — changes which view loads first and which historical observations appear in the summary. The demand data, KPIs and forecasts stay the same.

Overview What changed, and what matters now? Current demand, the next forecast peak, and recent model error.
US Grid How does demand compare across regions? Regional demand, interchange, and demand-to-nameplate comparisons in cards and maps.
Forecast What demand is forecast? Demand forecasts from Prophet, SARIMAX, XGBoost, and the ensemble, with labeled forecast ranges.
Risk Which alerts need review? Weather alerts, demand anomalies, and a demand-to-nameplate capacity comparison.
Models How have the models performed? Per-model holdout metrics, live drift, residuals, SHAP feature importance.

Forecast accuracy

Holdout backtests predict 168 hours recursively: each prediction feeds into the next. Results are published for all 51 balancing authorities. A separate live benchmark compares GridPulse with each operator’s day-ahead forecast on matched observations, with results per operator.

In the published recursive holdout, the served ensemble has a median per-BA error of 4.35%, compared with 3.69% for XGBoost alone. The ensemble has lower error on 21 of 51 authorities. Combining models does not consistently improve accuracy; the per-authority results show where it helps and where it does not.

Data sources and revisions

EIA publishes provisional readings that can revise heavily before settling. GridPulse measures each feed's revision behavior, classifies it live, and discloses exclusions in plain language on the dashboard.
