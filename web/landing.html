Forecast review workflow

Start with the forecast you are reviewing

  1. Identify the source

    Read the forecast name and published default. A selected model comparison may differ from the forecast currently published.

  2. Check the matching evidence

    Inspect observed error for that series, including the forecast horizon, measurement window, and scoring coverage. Keep training results and data limitations in view.

  3. Decide what to investigate

    Review the next demand peak, forecast curve, weather, and flagged observations to decide whether the forecast needs closer review.

Why it exists

What you can inspect

A forecast curve alone does not explain whether it needs attention. Review its source, observed error, demand history, and weather together, with update times and data warnings in view.

Demand and weather

Compare reported demand with forecasts and review the weather inputs used by the models.

Forecast error

Check observed error for the displayed series at a named forecast horizon and window, with scoring coverage. Horizon offsets run from forecast start; training evaluations are separate.

Weather alerts

Review active National Weather Service alerts and flagged demand observations for the selected region.

Weather scenarios

Adjust temperature, wind, or solar assumptions and inspect the resulting demand estimate. Each result labels its calculation method.

Dashboard views

What each view is for

Overview starts with the Forecast Review Analyst's decision. Renewables, trading, and data-science contexts offer other starting views and historical summaries using the same underlying data.

Overview Does this forecast need closer review? The published forecast, its matching observed error, the next demand peak, and reported-demand history.
US Grid How does demand compare across regions? Search reported demand and interchange, inspect an authority in the table or territory map, then review its forecast.
Forecast Which forecast am I inspecting? Start with the published default or select a model comparison. Source identity, matching error evidence, and forecast ranges stay visible.
Conditions What weather and demand context is available? Linked past, future and matched-error exploration, with UTC windows, exact values and separate weather messages.
Models How have the models performed? Per-model training evaluations, observed forecast error, residuals, and built-in XGBoost feature importance.

Forecast accuracy

Holdout backtests predict 168 hours recursively: each prediction feeds into the next. Results are published for all 51 balancing authorities. A separate live benchmark compares named model series with each operator’s day-ahead forecast on matched observations. The benchmark may measure a different series from the published default and does not score the fallback baseline.

In the published 2026-08-07 recursive holdout, the evaluated ensemble has a median per-BA error of 4.35%, compared with 3.69% for XGBoost alone. The ensemble has lower error on 21 of 51 authorities. Combining models does not consistently improve accuracy; the per-authority results show where it helps and where it does not.
